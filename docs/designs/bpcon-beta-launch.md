# Design: BPCON beta launch

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-13
Branch: master (v0.3.3.0)
Repo: lsouiher/investor-os
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup (pre-product: no users yet)

## Problem Statement

Launch InvestorOS as a free beta to ~600 contacts collected at last year's BiggerPockets
Conference (BPCON), most of whom will be back this year and most of whom will not remember
Leo. Goal: real usage and honest feedback, not compliments. The app works end to end on a
laptop against a mock AI; nothing is deployed and the real Anthropic run is unverified.

## Demand Evidence

- Strongest evidence so far: demos got "interesting". That is interest, not demand. Nobody
  has asked when it ships or been upset it isn't live. The launch's job is to convert
  interest into behavior we can count.
- Adjacent evidence that the job is real: the audience already pays. "Each one of them paid
  a few thousands to be there in the event just to learn a few strategies", and many pay
  coaches and masterminds on top. Money already moves for "which strategy is mine".

## Status Quo

They pay a coach or mastermind, go to BPCON, and come home with thirty strategies and no
way to know which one fits their money, time, skills, risk and horizon. Cost: thousands of
dollars a year, months of hesitation, and first deals run on someone else's strategy.

## Target User & Wedge

The room is not one person. Leo's words: "not only one to three doors. It's people who are
curious, zero doors, other people with more than three doors." The product has to give a
path to everyone, and it already does through one door:

| Segment | What they get in 20 minutes | Which part of the app |
|---|---|---|
| Curious, zero doors | What kind of investor they'd be, and the first move | Identity card, top strategy, 72-hour micro-plan |
| First doors, 1–3 | What is blocking door 4: capital, time, skills or network | Readiness radar, strategy, action plan |
| Scaling, 4+ | Growth paths for portfolio and capital from their own numbers | Growth Strategy (beta; two paths say Coming Soon) |

The wedge is the diagnosis everyone enters through, not one segment's feature:
**BPCON sells you thirty strategies. InvestorOS tells you which one is yours.**
The "hub to figure out your future" is the promise on the page; the sentence above is the
hook in the email.

## Constraints

- Free during beta; no payment, no pricing page.
- Audience is cold-to-warm: a conference contact list, not friends. The page must work
  for a stranger in ten seconds, on a phone.
- Today is 2026-09-13. BPCON 2026 is October 2–4 (day 1 is a Friday): 19 days out, so
  "T-3 weeks" is now and the schedule has no slack (see Schedule).
- Solo builder; Claude Code does the build, Leo does the copy, the sending, and the
  watching.
- Real users means real income and net-worth figures in the database, and a mostly US
  recipient list (CAN-SPAM) sent from Canada (CASL).

## Premises (agreed after revision)

1. There is no link to send yet. Deploying is the critical path.
2. The promise is "a hub for everyone to figure out their future"; the hook is one
   sentence that works for zero doors and twelve: which strategy is yours. (Revised from
   "the hook is the 1–3-door diagnosis" after Leo's pushback.)
3. A cold list of 600 needs a real landing page that explains the hub, plus a feedback loop
   inside the app and numbers on where people stop. (Revised from "no marketing site
   needed" after Leo's pushback: most of the 600 don't know him.)
4. Success is behavior, not compliments. Targets and floors are set before the first email.
5. Before the first stranger signs up: HTTPS, a beta terms-and-privacy page, a "not
   financial advice" line, working password reset, a delete-my-data path.

## Approaches Considered

- **A. Message + the app's existing landing page.** Fastest link; nothing on the page for a
  stranger or for the three segments; no feedback loop. Ruled out: burns the one shot at a
  cold list.
- **B. Real landing page + three-email sequence + in-app feedback + funnel numbers.**
  Chosen.
- **C. Event-native: the Identity Card as the hook at BPCON.** Kept as the stretch item
  (a shareable card image and an at-event "get your card in 20 minutes" moment) only if
  deploy is live on schedule at T-3. It needs B underneath it either way.

## Recommended Approach: B, with C as stretch

Peak-load assumption used for every sizing decision below: **50 people starting the app
within the same hour from the venue's Wi-Fi (one shared IP)**, on phones. Campaign totals
are far smaller (see Success Criteria); the venue hour is the spike.

### 1. Launch blockers (engineering, in this order)

Verified against the code on 2026-09-13; none of them is optional for real users.

1. **Anthropic credits, then the real-API pass.** Done 2026-09-13: $15 funded; enough for
   the real-API pass and roughly 30 full test runs, not for the campaign. Top up before
   Email 1 once the first ten real users show the per-user cost; set a monthly spend
   limit. Then run section R of the walkthrough (Leo's private checklist page, https://claude.ai/code/artifact/a02c6c7e-674e-421b-b4c5-0ca3cd3f80d1)
   **on the deployed host, not the laptop** (see blocker 2, proxy timeouts). Budget: roughly
   8–10 model calls per user who finishes; measure the first ten users. Working estimate for
   the campaign: low hundreds of dollars.
2. **Deploy.**
   - Decision (Leo: "figure it out"): **everything on Railway**, one account, one bill:
     a Next.js service, the Express API + in-process Bull worker (`main.ts:97`) as a
     Docker service, Railway Postgres and Redis. Railway does not cut long requests the
     way Cloudflare-fronted hosts do, and one platform means one login for a solo
     founder. Puppeteer and the in-process worker rule out serverless anyway.
     **There is no Dockerfile in the repo yet**;
     add `backend/Dockerfile` on a Chromium-capable base for Puppeteer 24, or hide the
     Blueprint PDF button for the beta. Managed Postgres with daily backups; managed
     Redis; one domain with HTTPS.
   - Production env checklist: `DATABASE_URL`, `REDIS_URL`, `JWT_SECRET`,
     `AUDIT_ENCRYPTION_KEY_V1`, `CURRENT_ENCRYPTION_KEY_VERSION`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`
     (+ `ANTHROPIC_WORKSPACE_ID` only for org-level keys), `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`,
     `CORS_ORIGIN` = the exact origin users see in the address bar (`main.ts:34`; add the
     `*.vercel.app` preview origin comma-separated if you use it), `NODE_ENV=production`;
     frontend `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL` with the `/api/v1` prefix. Run `prisma migrate deploy` and the
     prompt-template seed; `/health` must report `ai:true`.
   - **Escrow the secrets before deploy** (`JWT_SECRET`, both encryption vars): losing
     `AUDIT_ENCRYPTION_KEY_V1` makes every income and credit field unreadable, and a
     database backup does not contain it.
   - **Long requests, unconditional fix.** Three calls in the 20-minute flow hold an HTTP
     connection while the model works: `POST /identity/synthesize` (up to 180 s),
     `GET /strategies` right after synthesis (joins the in-flight generation, up to 180 s)
     and `POST /simulations` (up to 120 s). Host proxies cut idle connections at 60–100 s
     (Cloudflare in front of Render: 100 s) and iOS Safari drops a request that receives
     no bytes for about 60 s, so this fails on phones on every host. Fix before the
     real-API pass, no new endpoints needed: synthesis already runs server-side when the
     fifth audit completes, so the identity page fires the POST and polls `GET /identity`
     until a new version appears; `GET /strategies` returns `{ generating: true }` while
     generation is in flight and the page polls; simulation shows progress and polls its
     result. Strategy activation already works this way (plan generation is
     fire-and-forget, the page polls). Railway and Fly are the safer hosts for whatever
     stays long.
   - **`trust proxy` is hard-coded to one hop** (`main.ts:29`). Behind two hops
     (Cloudflare → Render) every user shares the CDN's IP and one rate-limit bucket. On the
     deploy checklist: load the app from a phone and confirm the host log shows the
     phone's IP, not the CDN's; adjust the hop count.
3. **Shared-Wi-Fi rate limits.** Two IP-keyed limiters lock the venue out:
   `generalLimiter` (100 requests/min per IP, mounted globally at `main.ts:39`) gives 50
   people 2 requests a minute each, and `authLimiter` (10 per 15 min per IP on register and
   login) stops the eleventh signup. `generalLimiter` is mounted before `authenticate`
   (`main.ts:39` vs `:78`), so keying it by user there is a no-op. Fix: a public IP limiter
   with a much higher ceiling on the unauthenticated routes (`/auth`, `/health`), and a
   per-user limiter (`userOrIpKey`, `rate-limiter.ts:5`) mounted after `authenticate` on
   the authenticated chain; register at 100 per 15 min per IP; login at 10 per 15 min per
   IP + email (brute-force protection stays); forgot-password keyed by IP + email. Also:
   the AI client sends `maxRetries: 0`, so a 429 from Anthropic during the venue hour fails
   that user's request outright; retry 429/529 twice inside the existing deadline, and
   during the real-API pass fire ten syntheses in parallel to see the account's rate tier.
   Cheap, same worker file: `pathGenerationQueue.process(5, handler)`
   (`growth-path-worker.ts:40`; today one job at a time, ~3 min each) so the venue hour
   doesn't queue growth paths for two hours. Cross-path queue unchanged.
4. **Password reset that sends email.** Endpoints exist; the email is a `TODO` at
   `auth/service.ts:86` (token logged only in development, silent no-op in production), and
   there are no forgot/reset pages. Wire Resend and add the two pages. Strangers forget
   passwords in week one.
5. **Growth Strategy on by default for new tenants.** Decision: on. The 4+ segment's value
   is the growth paths, and honest Coming Soon cards beat a missing feature. Cost: with the
   flag on, synthesis auto-creates the strategy and queues one Portfolio generation in the
   background (one extra model call per user, no wait for the user). Add an env default so
   registration sets `growth_strategy_enabled: true` instead of per-tenant SQL.
6. **Dashboard insights hit the model on every load.** `generateInsights`
   (`insight/service.ts`) calls Claude with no cache and the dashboard route awaits it, so
   every dashboard visit costs a model call and waits 15–30 s to render. Fix: store the
   generated insights per user, return them instantly, regenerate in the background at
   most every 6 hours or when a strategy, task or contact changes. Without this the
   dashboard is the slowest page in the app and the biggest line on the bill.
7. **Beta terms and privacy page** (one page): what is stored, which fields are encrypted,
   never sold, deleted on request; and the line "InvestorOS is a planning tool, not
   financial, legal or tax advice", repeated on the strategies page. Footer link from
   landing, register and login. **Delete-my-data path (new script):** `npm run
   delete-user -- <email>` run by Leo against production; it nulls the encrypted audit
   fields immediately and soft-deletes the user and tenant, so the page can honestly say
   "your financial data is erased on request".
8. **Feedback loop.** "Feedback" link in the sidebar footer (mailto), and a free-text
   "what did we get wrong or miss?" box under the existing 1–5 rating after the identity
   reveal, stored next to the rating.
9. **Funnel numbers, in SQL.** Registration stores `signup_source` from `utm_source`
   (`bpcon` for email, `qr` for the event). Nothing reads a query param today, and the
   landing page's "Start free" link drops the query string, so the landing page stashes
   `utm_source` in `sessionStorage` and the register call sends it in the body. A daily
   report script counts registered → all five audits complete → identity revealed →
   strategy activated → growth created, and week-two returns from `last_login_at`. No
   analytics vendor.
10. **Mobile pass, before the watch sessions.** Complete the 20-minute flow on iOS Safari
    and Android Chrome on the deployed host; Email 2 and the event are phone-first and
    everything so far was tested on a laptop. Done first, so the T-2 watch sessions surface
    product feedback instead of layout bugs.
11. **Monitoring.** Host log drain plus `/health`; no error-tracking vendor for a
    two-week beta of under a hundred users.

### 2. Landing page (rebuild `/`)

Written for a stranger who just got an email, on a phone. Leo writes the final words; the
structure:

- **Hero.** "Figure out your future in real estate." Sub: "Twenty minutes of honest
  questions. Your investor identity, the strategies that fit it, and a plan for the next 72
  hours. Free during the BPCON beta." Buttons: Start free · See how it works.
- **Three paths** (the segment table above, one card each, same size, same voice).
- **How it works.** 5 audits → identity card (archetype + readiness radar) → 3 strategies
  ranked by fit → activate one → action plan, roadmap, 72-hour micro-plan → growth paths
  (beta).
- **What you get.** Four real screenshots: identity card, radar, strategy detail, micro-plan.
- **Built by.** Leo, investor, city, one sentence on why (last year's notebook full of
  strategies). A face and a name are the only social proof available; use them.
- **FAQ.** Is it free (yes, during beta). How long (about 20 minutes; you can stop and come
  back). Is my financial data safe (encrypted fields, never sold, deleted on request). Is
  this advice (no; planning tool). What does beta mean (two growth paths say Coming Soon;
  you will hit rough edges; tell me).
- **Footer.** Beta terms & privacy · Contact.
- English only for BPCON. The site's FR toggle stays; missing `landing.*` French keys fall
  back to English, so no one stalls on translation.
- Links carry `?utm_source=bpcon` (emails) and `?utm_source=qr` (event); the page stashes
  it and registration stores it (blocker 9).

### 3. The three emails and the event pitch

- **Sender setup at T-3:** a real domain, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records, and a sending tool
  with unsubscribe links (Resend Broadcasts or similar; a Gmail merge caps out around 500
  a day and has no unsubscribe handling). Send a test to a seed inbox at Gmail, Outlook and
  iCloud before the list.
- **Ramp, don't blast:** the ~50 contacts Leo actually knows first, then ~100 a day. A
  fresh domain sending 600 in one burst lands in spam, and the open target dies there.
- **List hygiene at T-3:** dedupe, drop entries without a first name (or use "Hi there"),
  and remove bounces after the first send.
- **Every email** ends with Leo's name, a physical mailing address (CAN-SPAM; the list is
  mostly US) and the tool's unsubscribe link (also CASL: identify yourself, give a way out;
  consent implied by a conference contact exchange is time-limited). The "reply no" line
  stays as a courtesy on top of the link.

## Success Criteria

Targets to beat, set before Email 1, from 600 emails: 40% open, 15% click (~90), 60% of
clickers register (~55), **60% of registrants finish all five audits (~33)**, 70% of
finishers activate a strategy (~23), 30% of finishers come back in week two (~10), at
least 5 substantive replies, at least 3 people asking to pay or to bring a partner.

Floors, so a deliverability miss is not misread as product failure: 20% open, 5% click
(~30), 50% of clickers register (~15), 50% of those finish (~8). Below the open/click
floors the problem is the list or inbox placement; below the finish floor it is the
product. Audit completion is the number that decides whether the 20-minute ask is worth it
to a stranger; everything downstream depends on it.

their words, not yours. Do this by T-2 weeks.
